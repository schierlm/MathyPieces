Continued Fractions

Continued fractions are a form to examine real numbers. Finite continued fractions represent rational numbers, and periodic continued fractions are a solution of a rational quadratic equation. This page can take any Constructive Real and compute its continued fraction, detecting whether it is finite or periodic.

Expression Guesser

Find, for a given Constructed Real, whether there is a simple expression that produces it. Supports expressions of the forms x, √x, ∛x, xπ, xπ², x/π, x/π², ex, 2x, 10x, ln x, log2 x and log10 x, where x is either rational or has a periodic continued fraction. In case the expression is an integer, show its prime factorization as well.

Arbitrary Bases

Most people know bases like decimal or hexadecimal, others have already computed in bases like ternary, but did you know you can use any real number larger than 1 as as base (yes, base π is possible). Convert any Constructive Real to any base given by a second Constructive Real. However, bases larger than 36 are not supported to keep the set of digits simple. Note that, technically, negative bases (smaller than -1) are also possible, but are not (yet?) supported by this tool.

Negative Bases And Nonstandard Base Digits

Inspired by this YouTube video, you can experiment here with negative (integer) bases and/or negative-valued digits (like symmetric ternary), but can get totally crazy with choosing which digit values you want, just like demonstrated in the video.
